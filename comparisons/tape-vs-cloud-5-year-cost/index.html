Tape vs Cloud: The Five-Year Cost of an Archive

Over five years, 100 TB costs about $5,532 in AWS S3 Glacier Deep Archive against $8,254 to $14,169 for one LTO-9 tape copy including the drive, and at 500 TB tape costs $17,987 to $30,123 against $27,660 for cloud.

The numbers

Five-year cost of keeping an archive, September 2026 list prices

Tape here is LTO-9 at $92.45 to $111.55 per 18 TB cartridge plus a new internal drive at $7,699 to $13,500 (two drives from 500 TB up, so a copy can be read back while another is written). Cloud is AWS S3 Glacier Deep Archive at $0.00099 per GB-month in US East (N. Virginia), one copy, kept for the full five years. The restore column is what it costs to pull the whole archive out of Glacier once, at bulk retrieval plus internet egress.

Reading the table

Cloud wins small, tape wins big

At 100 TB, cloud archive is cheaper over five years. A 100 TB archive costs about $5,532 in Glacier Deep Archive for five years, while one tape copy costs $8,254 to $14,169, most of it the drive. The tape only pays for itself after 7.5 to 12.8 years at that size.

Around 500 TB the two meet. One tape copy of 500 TB costs $17,987 to $30,123 against $27,660 for five years of Glacier, so tape is clearly cheaper at the low end of drive pricing and about level if you pay top of market. It breaks even in 3.3 to 5.4 years. At 1 PB the drive is a rounding error: media alone is $5,177 to $6,247, a one-off cost against $55,321 of storage fees.

The reason is simple. A tape drive is a fixed cost that does not care how much data you have; cartridges then cost about $5.14 to $6.20 per TB once. Cloud archive has no entry cost and bills every month forever.

Restores

The number that changes the answer

Reading an archive back from tape costs drive time. Reading it back from cloud archive costs money: pulling 100 TB out of Glacier Deep Archive is about $8,615 at bulk retrieval ($0.0025 per GB) plus internet egress ($0.09 per GB), and standard retrieval is $0.02 per GB instead of $0.0025.

Two details decide whether that number ever lands on your invoice:

  • Retrieval tier. Bulk retrieval from Deep Archive takes up to 48 hours. If you need it faster you pay the standard rate, which is 8 times more per GB.
  • Where the data goes. Egress is free to other services inside the same provider and charged when it leaves for the internet, so a restore into your own data centre costs full egress.

Azure Archive charges the same $0.00099 per GB-month as Deep Archive but has no bulk tier: retrieving 1 TB is $18.63 at standard priority against $2.33 on AWS, before egress.

US East (N. Virginia) list prices before committed-use discounts, taken from each provider's own pricing data on 2026-09-20. Notes that matter when you model this yourself:

  • AWS, Azure and Google all bill storage in binary gigabytes (230 bytes, also called GiB). A decimal terabyte of tape data is about 931 billed gigabytes, and every cloud figure on this page accounts for that.
  • Wasabi and Backblaze include egress only within a policy: Wasabi while monthly egress stays at or below what you store, Backblaze up to three times your average stored data.
  • Minimum storage duration bites on deletion. Delete from Deep Archive after a month and you still pay the remaining 180 days; Google Archive charges a full year.
  • AWS adds 40 KB of metadata to every archived object, so millions of small files cost far more than their raw size. Pack them into larger archives before upload.

What the table leaves out

Costs on both sides

Tape: a SAS HBA per drive, somewhere climate-controlled to keep cartridges, courier or vault fees for the offsite copy, backup software or LTFS tooling, a drive refresh every several years, and staff time to load, verify and label media. Budget one migration during a long retention period, because drives only read one generation back (see LTO tape migration).

Cloud: upload requests, minimum storage duration, early-deletion fees, retrieval tiers, egress, and the chance that list prices change during a ten-year retention. The full restore cost belongs in the budget even if you never plan to use it, because that is what a disaster looks like.

Both: the staging disk at each end, and the second copy you should keep either way. Most teams end up with disk for recent restores, tape for offline retention and a cloud copy for site loss, which is the 3-2-1 split rather than a single winner.

Is tape cheaper than cloud storage?

It depends on size and retention. At 100 TB over five years, AWS S3 Glacier Deep Archive costs about $5,532 against $8,254 to $14,169 for one LTO-9 tape copy including a drive, so cloud is cheaper. At 500 TB tape costs $17,987 to $30,123 against $27,660 for cloud, so tape is level or cheaper, and by 1 PB it is less than half the cost.

What does it cost to restore 100 TB from Glacier Deep Archive?

About $8,615 at September 2026 list prices: $0.0025 per GB for bulk retrieval plus $0.09 per GB of internet egress. Standard retrieval costs $0.02 per GB instead.

How long before a tape drive pays for itself?

At 100 TB, 7.5 to 12.8 years against Glacier Deep Archive. At 500 TB, 3.3 to 5.4 years. The drive is a fixed cost, so the more you store the faster it is repaid.

How many LTO-9 tapes is 1 PB?

56 cartridges for one copy at 18 TB native each, about $5,177 to $6,247 of media. Compression does not help for already-compressed archive data.

Which cloud archive tier is cheapest?

AWS S3 Glacier Deep Archive has the lowest headline rate here at $0.00099 per GB-month, and AWS is also far cheaper to read back because it offers a bulk retrieval tier. Wasabi and Backblaze cost more per month and include egress within policy limits.
