LTO‑10 Price and Compatibility Guide

US street prices for LTO-10 drives and 30TB or 40TB media on 23 October 2026, compatibility limits, and per-TB math against LTO-9 for tape buyers.

On our October 2026 price data, LTO-10 still costs more per terabyte than LTO-9, so most tape buyers save money by staying on LTO-9. Our tapebackup.org price tracker from 23 October 2026 shows LTO-10 30TB media at 8,63 $ to 10,00 $ per native TB and external drives from 23.422 $, while LTO-9 media runs 5,14 $ to 7,17 $ per TB. LTO-10 only pays off when library slot count or cartridge handling outweighs media cost.

LTO‑10 drive and cartridge prices on 23 October 2026

Our tapebackup.org price tracker recorded US street prices across major resellers on 23 October 2026. LTO-10 30TB cartridges are broadly in stock at 259,00 $ to 299,99 $, which equals 8,63 $ to 10,00 $ per native TB. LTO-10 40TB cartridges cost 491,00 $ to 555,00 $, or 12,28 $ to 13,88 $ per native TB, with Fujifilm 40TB in stock at MagStor, limited at Tape4Backup, and special order at TapeandMedia.

LTO-10 30TB WORM cartridges were out of stock across all four brands at Tape4Backup on 23 October 2026, with out-of-stock list prices running 420,00 $ to 460,80 $ (14,00 $ to 15,36 $ per native TB). No LTO-10 40TB WORM cartridge appeared at any seller we checked. By comparison, LTO-9 18TB cartridges cost 92,45 $ to 129,00 $ on 23 October 2026, or 5,14 $ to 7,17 $ per native TB.

Hardware pricing widens the gap further. On 23 October 2026, external LTO-10 drives ranged from 23.422 $ to 24.950 $ in our tracker, compared with 9.199 $ to 15.225 $ for external LTO-9 drives. At BackupWorks, a Qualstar Q-Series LTO-10 SAS full-height library add-on drive (800-0110-0) was listed at 28.068 $ on 23 October 2026, up from 23.509 $ in September 2026. Tape4Backup also continued to display its banner on 23 October 2026 stating that all LTO tape prices increased from 1 September 2026.

Media math: 300 TB native on LTO‑9 vs LTO‑10

A simple worked example using the lowest street prices in our 23 October 2026 tracker shows why LTO-9 wins on pure media spend for a 300 TB native archive:

  • LTO-9 (18 TB cartridges at 92,45 $): 300 TB divided by 18 TB equals 16,67, so you buy 17 cartridges (306 TB total). 17 multiplied by 92,45 $ equals 1.571,65 $.
  • LTO-10 30TB (at 259,00 $): 300 TB divided by 30 TB equals 10 cartridges. 10 multiplied by 259,00 $ equals 2.590,00 $, which is 1.018,35 $ more than LTO-9.
  • LTO-10 40TB (at 491,00 $): 300 TB divided by 40 TB equals 7,5, so you buy 8 cartridges (320 TB total). 8 multiplied by 491,00 $ equals 3.928,00 $, which is 2.356,35 $ more than LTO-9.

Add the drive cost and the difference grows. Comparing the lowest external LTO-10 drive (23.422 $) against the lowest external LTO-9 drive (9.199 $) in our 23 October 2026 tracker adds 14.223 $ upfront. In our reading, LTO-10 only makes sense today when physical slot count, library rack space, or cartridge handling time matters more than cost per TB. Why 40TB LTO‑10 exists and what changed

Blocks & Files reported on 13 November 2025 that the LTO consortium raised LTO-10 raw capacity to 40 TB, a 33 percent increase over the 30 TB cartridges initially delivered by Fujifilm and Sony with sole drive supplier IBM. LTO-10 had originally been specified for 36 TB native, and the 40 TB upgrade uses a thinner, smoother aramid-based tape ribbon substrate in place of polyethylene naphthalate (PEN) film so longer tape fits inside the standard Ultrium shell. In the same announcement, the consortium lowered its LTO-14 target to 365 TB raw (913 TB compressed) from 576 TB raw (1,44 PB compressed), a shift we covered in our LTO roadmap analysis.

TechRadar reported in September 2026 that Fujifilm began selling its 40TB LTO-10 cartridge, which reaches 100 TB at standard 2,5 to 1 compression and uses Barium Ferrite particles across 15.105 tracks. TechRadar noted that Fujifilm rates the cartridge at 440MB per second uncompressed (1.100MB per second compressed), a bit error rate of 1 in 10 to the 20th power, FIPS 197 compliant AES-256 encryption, and an archival life of up to 30 years under proper storage conditions. We also tracked the supply side in our Fujifilm LTO production update.

Compatibility limits and hardware availability

LTO-10 drives cannot read or write older LTO generations, as documented in our 23 October 2026 price tracker notes. If you hold an LTO-9 archive, an LTO-10 drive cannot restore those tapes, so you must keep at least one LTO-9 drive online or migrate the data before retiring older hardware.

For 40TB media, our 23 October 2026 tracker notes confirm that existing full-height LTO-10 drives accept the 40TB cartridge, while IBM states that its own 40TB cartridges (models 564 and 664, announced 6 March 2026 in 20- and 5-packs with no street price seen) work only in IBM LTO-10 drives. Hardware choice also remains narrow on 23 October 2026: only full-height LTO-10 drives are on the market, no half-height LTO-10 drive was listed at any seller we checked (Symply had expected half-height in Q2 2026 and the LTO Program said late 2026), and no bare internal standalone LTO-10 drive price was listed at the HPE, IBM, or Quantum resellers we checked. Domande frequenti

Can an LTO-10 drive read or write LTO-9 tapes?

No. Per our 23 October 2026 price tracker notes, LTO-10 drives cannot read or write any older LTO generation, including LTO-9.

How much does LTO-10 media cost per TB compared to LTO-9?

On 23 October 2026, tapebackup.org tracked LTO-9 18TB media at 5,14 $ to 7,17 $ per native TB, LTO-10 30TB media at 8,63 $ to 10,00 $ per native TB, and LTO-10 40TB media at 12,28 $ to 13,88 $ per native TB.

Do 40TB LTO-10 cartridges work in existing LTO-10 drives?

Yes, existing full-height LTO-10 drives accept the 40TB cartridge, though IBM specifies that its own 40TB cartridges work only in IBM LTO-10 drives, according to our 23 October 2026 tracker notes.

Are half-height or internal LTO-10 drives available yet?

No half-height LTO-10 drive and no bare internal standalone LTO-10 drive price appeared at any reseller checked by tapebackup.org on 23 October 2026, with the LTO Program pointing to late 2026 for half-height availability.

When is LTO-10 worth buying over LTO-9?

In our reading, LTO-10 makes sense when library slot count, rack space, or cartridge handling matters more than media cost per TB, because both LTO-10 media and external drives (23.422 $ to 24.950 $ on 23 October 2026) cost substantially more than LTO-9.
