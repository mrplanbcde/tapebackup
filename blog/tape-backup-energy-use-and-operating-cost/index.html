Tape Backup Energy Use and Operating Cost

Which tape backup options are the most energy efficient, and how do they compare with disk and cloud on operating cost? A 500 TB model with assumptions.

A standalone LTO drive that is switched on only while it writes is the most energy-efficient way to keep an archive: a cartridge on a shelf uses no power at all. In our 500 TB model, a disk array kept online uses about 4,400 kWh a year, a small tape library left on all year about 800 kWh, and a standalone drive used only for writing about 16 kWh.

Energy figures depend on your hardware, so below are the assumptions behind each number. Swap in the values from your own data sheets to redo the sums.

Operating cost per year

At $0.13 per kWh, close to the US commercial average, the power bill is small for tape and noticeable for disk. For cloud archive storage, the monthly bill is the operating cost.

Other operating costs to plan for

Power is only part of the picture. Disk arrays need drives replaced every three to seven years. Tape needs cleaning cartridges, offsite storage for the second copy, a periodic read check and a migration to newer media every two or three drive generations. Cloud adds retrieval and egress fees whenever you read data back.

Frequently asked questions

Does tape use power when it is stored?

No. A cartridge on a shelf uses no electricity. Only the drive or library uses power, and only while it is switched on.

Is a tape library worth its extra power?

Usually yes if jobs span several cartridges or run unattended. A small library adds tens of watts of idle draw, which costs roughly $100 a year in our model.

How can I cut tape energy further?

Power down standalone drives between jobs, eject cartridges to the shelf, and schedule large writes together so the drive streams without stopping.

Is cloud storage more energy efficient than tape?

You cannot measure it directly: the energy is inside the provider's price and data centre. For cold data, tape on a shelf uses less energy than any always-on system.
