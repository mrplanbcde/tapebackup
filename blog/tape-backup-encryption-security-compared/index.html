Tape Encryption and Security, Side by Side

Which tape backup systems have the best encryption and security? AES-256 in LTO drives, key management options, WORM and air gap compared side by side.

Every LTO drive from LTO-4 onward encrypts in hardware with AES-256, whichever brand is on the front, so the drive is not what separates one tape system from another. What differs is how the encryption keys are managed: by the backup software, by the tape library through an external key manager, or by the host's tape driver. Add WORM cartridges and a copy that is offline when ejected, and tape covers the threats disk and cloud copies struggle with.

What every current LTO drive has

Encryption, write-once media and the LTFS file system arrived in different generations and are part of the LTO standard, so they behave the same on HPE, IBM, Quantum and Dell drives of the same generation.

The three ways to manage keys, compared

MethodWhere the key livesGood forWatch out for
Application-managedThe backup software's databaseOne backup product, simple setupsKeys are tied to that software. Back up its key store, or a lost database means unreadable tapes
Library-managedAn external key manager the library reaches over KMIPSeveral applications sharing a library, audits and complianceNeeds a key manager that is always reachable; on some library models it is a licensed feature
System-managedA key manager reached by the host's tape device driverIBM environmentsMostly limited to IBM drivers and platforms

Whichever method you choose, the rule is the same: an encrypted cartridge without its key cannot be read, by you or anyone else. Keep the keys in at least two places, separate from the tapes.

Frequently asked questions

Does tape encryption reduce capacity or speed?

Not noticeably. The drive compresses data first and then encrypts it in hardware, so native and compressed capacity stay the same.

Which LTO generations support hardware encryption?

LTO-4 and every generation since, including LTO-9 and LTO-10, with AES-256.

Is WORM the same as encryption?

No. WORM stops data from being changed or deleted; encryption stops it from being read without the key. For ransomware protection you usually want both.

What happens if I lose the encryption key?

The data on that cartridge cannot be recovered. Store keys in a key manager or in your backup software's protected key store, with a copy kept away from the tapes.
